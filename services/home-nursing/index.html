Home Nursing

Some care needs clinical skill: injections, wound dressing, catheter care, checking vitals. For this we send nurses with valid nursing-council registration, who work only on a doctor’s written instructions.

Who it’s for

  • Patients discharged from hospital with a doctor’s home-care plan
  • Families who need injections, dressings or catheter care at home
  • Anyone whose doctor has prescribed regular monitoring of vitals

What’s included

  • InjectionsAs prescribed
  • Wound care and dressingAs prescribed
  • Monitoring vitals
  • IV and catheter careAs prescribed

Clear limits

Our nurses follow the doctor’s written instruction and do not change treatment on their own. We work alongside your doctor or hospital, not in place of them.

We are not a hospital and do not promise medical outcomes. In an emergency, call 108 / 102.

How we keep it safe

  • Every nurse’s council registration number and validity is checked before they start
  • The registration number is written into your agreement
  • Clinical tasks follow the doctor’s written instruction
  • Supervisor check-ins and a daily duty log, as with all our staff

Duty options

  • 8-hour duty

    Standard shift with one weekly off.

  • 12-hour duty

    8 hours plus 4 hours of overtime, billed at the overtime rate.

  • 24-hour care (rotation)

    Two staff in rotation, or one with at least 8 hours’ rest including uninterrupted sleep.

Questions families ask

Do I need a nurse or a caretaker?

If your doctor has prescribed a clinical procedure at home — injections, IV, dressings, catheter care — you need a nurse. If the main need is help with daily living, a caretaker is usually right. Not sure? Describe the situation and we’ll tell you honestly.

What do you need from us before a nurse starts?

The doctor’s written instructions or discharge summary, the list of medicines, and your doctor’s or hospital’s contact details.

Share the doctor’s advice with us.

A short conversation is enough to know what kind of help fits. No obligation.
