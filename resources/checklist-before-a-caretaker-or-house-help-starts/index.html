Before a caretaker or house help starts: a safety checklist for Jammu families

Whether you find help through a neighbour, an agency or Movira Care, these are the checks worth doing before anyone starts work in your home — and the house rules that keep everyone safe afterwards.

By Movira Care Team26 November 20264 min read

Most families in Jammu find help the way their parents did: a neighbour knows someone, a relative recommends a cook, a caretaker’s cousin needs work. Often it works out well. But the person who walks into your home will be alone with your parent, your child or your belongings, sometimes for many hours a day. A few careful checks at the start protect your family — and treat the worker fairly too.

Use this checklist whoever you hire.

1. Identity and age

  • See an original photo ID and check the face, name and address match. Keep a copy only with their agreement, and store it safely.
  • Confirm they are 18 or older. Employing children is illegal, and no child should be doing care work.
  • Note a permanent address and a family contact, not just a mobile number.
  • Never keep someone’s original documents. Hold copies only. Keeping originals traps people in jobs and is not acceptable.

2. Police verification

Police verification of domestic workers is done through the local police. Ask at your nearest police station, or check the official J&K Police channels, for the current process and the form they need. Do it before work starts, not “once they settle in”.

A worker who refuses verification, or keeps postponing it, is telling you something.

3. References — and actually call them

Ask for two people they have worked for before, and phone both. Ask specific questions:

  • How long did they work for you, and what exactly did they do?
  • Were they on time? How did they handle a bad day?
  • Did anything ever go missing, or did anything worry you?
  • Why did the work end?
  • Would you have them back?

Listen for hesitation as much as for words. A neighbour who “has heard good things” is not a reference.

4. Health

Anyone caring for an older person, a patient or a baby should be fit for the work and free of contagious illness. Ask for a doctor’s fitness certificate, and for screening for contagious illnesses such as TB. This protects the people they care for — and the worker.

5. Skills for the job

  • For a caretaker: have they lifted, turned or bathed an adult safely before? Ask them to explain how.
  • For baby care: how do they make a feed, settle a crying baby, handle a fever? Never leave a newborn alone with someone on day one.
  • For a cook: a trial meal tells you more than any conversation. Explain clearly about veg, non-veg, Jain or a doctor’s diet.
  • For a nurse: ask for the nursing-council registration number and check that it is valid. A uniform is not a registration.

6. Put the work in writing

Most disputes start with “but I thought…”. Before day one, write down and agree:

  • The tasks — and the tasks that are not included
  • The hours, the weekly off and what happens on holidays
  • The pay, when it is paid and how
  • What happens if they are ill, need leave or want to stop
  • Who they call if something goes wrong

House rules that protect everyone

  • Lock away cash, jewellery and valuables. Even the most honest worker should not have to face suspicion if something is misplaced.
  • No loans or advances. They are the most common cause of falling out.
  • Cameras: be open about them. If you use CCTV, tell the worker, and never place cameras in bathrooms or rooms where they change or sleep.
  • Visitors and phones. Agree whether friends or family can visit, and when phone calls are fine.
  • For children: who may collect your child, whether they may take your child out, and the rule on photos and social media.
  • Emergency numbers. Write down 108 or 112, your number, a neighbour’s number and the doctor’s number, and keep them by the phone.

Red flags

  • Asks for a large advance before starting
  • Won’t give references, or gives only phone numbers that never answer
  • Refuses police verification or a health check
  • Different names or addresses on different documents
  • An agency that takes a fee from the worker or keeps their original documents — if they treat workers that way, think about how they will treat you

How Movira Care handles these checks

If you would rather not do all of this yourself, this is what we do before anyone we employ is sent to a home:

  • Identity and age (18+)
  • Police verification
  • Two references and previous work history
  • A doctor’s fitness certificate and contagious-illness screening
  • Training in hygiene, safe lifting, first aid, safeguarding and privacy
  • For nurses, a check of their nursing-council registration, which is written into your agreement

Staff are employed and paid by us, and we never take any fee, deposit or commission from them. Every assignment comes with a written list of tasks and hours.

We will also be honest: verification reduces risk, it cannot remove it. Please still lock away cash and valuables, whoever you hire.
